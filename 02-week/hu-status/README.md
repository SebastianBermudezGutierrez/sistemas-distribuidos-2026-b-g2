## 2. My individual contribution
- I looked into the main things that define Scrum: the roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers), the events (Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Retrospective), the artifacts (Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment) and how the Sprint cycle works.
- I also researched Kanban: how it works with a continuous flow instead of sprints, the use of WIP limits, and the idea of constantly improving the flow.
- With that info I put together a table comparing both methodologies in things like approach, duration, planning, how they handle changes, roles, meetings, the board, delivery and metrics.
- I added the metrics both share and some notes on when it makes more sense to use one or the other.
- At the end I added a small conclusion: Scrum and Kanban aren't really rivals, they can be combined, and the best choice depends on the type of project and the team.
